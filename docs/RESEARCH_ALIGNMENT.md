# Research / Standards Alignment Notes

This project is not formally endorsed or certified by the Council of Europe or Cambridge English. The sources below are used as external reference points for curriculum and assessment design.

## CEFR

The Council of Europe describes CEFR levels through illustrative can-do descriptors across communicative activities and strategies. C1 includes ability with demanding longer texts, implicit meaning, flexible use of language, fluent/spontaneous expression and clear well-structured production.

Primary references:

- `https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors`
- `https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale/`
- `https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-3-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-qualitative-aspects-of-spoken-language-use`
- `https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/tests-and-examinations`

The project therefore separates skill profiles and uses productive/interactive assessment rather than a single internal English score.

## Guided-learning workload

Cambridge English currently publishes approximate guidance of roughly 200 guided learning hours per CEFR level and approximately 700–800 cumulative guided hours from complete beginner to C1, while explicitly stating that individual requirements vary.

Reference:

- `https://support.cambridgeenglish.org/hc/en-gb/articles/202838506-Guided-learning-hours`

The learner in this project is not a complete beginner. The shipped curriculum contains approximately 618.8 scheduled structured hours. This is treated as a substantial workload pathway, not proof of attainment. Mastery gates and independent final assessment remain mandatory.
